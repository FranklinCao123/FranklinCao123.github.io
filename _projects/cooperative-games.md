## Overview

This ongoing project explores cooperative game valuation through the lens of statistical mechanics. It brings together entropy-based modeling, variational inference, and the study of collective behavior to investigate the foundations of valuation methods relevant to machine learning.

## Background

Cooperative game theory provides a framework for assigning value to participants whose contributions depend on collaboration. In machine learning, this perspective has been applied to valuing training data, as illustrated by [Data Shapley (Ghorbani and Zou, ICML 2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/ghorbani19c.html).

An energy-based formulation offers a connection to statistical physics: coalitions are treated as configurations of a probabilistic system, and valuation is studied through variational inference. [Bian et al. (ICLR 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02938) develop this framework, relating established game-theoretic valuation criteria to mean-field inference and introducing the Variational Index.

## Research Questions

How does the structure of cooperation influence the behavior of an energy-based valuation system? How should collective effects, stability, and sensitivity to modeling assumptions be understood? When does a simplified approximation faithfully represent the underlying coalition model?

These questions motivate a statistical-mechanical investigation of valuation, with particular attention to the distinction between properties of the original probabilistic model and those introduced by approximation.

## Approach

The project combines analytical study of representative cooperative games with numerical exploration. Entropy, free-energy landscapes, symmetry, and mean-field methods provide complementary tools for examining how individual contributions relate to collective behavior.

The broader aim is to develop a clearer physical understanding of cooperative valuation and its potential relevance to machine-learning applications.
